\subsection{Phase C: Structural Mutation via a Semantic Guard}
\label{sec:exp:phase-c}

Phase B closed the repair loop---bypass, proposal, sandbox acceptance,
regression lock, re-measurement---entirely inside one representational space,
the space of lexical surface form. That loop works: it permanently closes the
encoding family of mutations (Base64, hex, ROT13, zero-width separators,
full-width Latin) and it keeps the benign false-positive rate flat. It also
stops. In the official Phase B run
(\texttt{run-20261028-185512}), the evolved defense reaches its held-out
plateau of 0.775 (31/40) at generation~1 and stays there through generation~19;
over that span thirteen further patches are accepted (P-0003 through P-0015),
the training-stream interception rate keeps climbing to 0.90, and the held-out
rate gains exactly nothing, while the benign false-positive rate remains
constant at 0.027 (2/74). The nine residual held-out misses are all
language-class: English paraphrases with no encoded string and no foreign
script, Russian and Arabic items rewritten with vocabulary that the added
tokens never covered. Phase~B's own evidence therefore locates the boundary:
more lexical patches consume more surface forms, but a paraphrase that
preserves meaning while destroying surface evidence is not a string-level
problem at all.

Phase~C asks what changes when the controller stops adding tokens and changes
the space it regulates in. The experiment is deliberately narrow: keep the two
lexical systems fixed, add one semantic channel, and measure all three with a
single instrument on two held-out variant sets.

\paragraph{Three systems under one instrument.} The three systems are defined
once in the contract and are not re-specified per analysis:
$S_A$ is the Phase~A lexical gate (\texttt{phase\_b\_defense.scan(text, None)});
$S_B$ is $S_A$ with the evolved patch snapshot of the Phase~B official run
(\texttt{scan(text, evolved\_patch\_snapshot.json)}); $S_C$ is $S_B$ \emph{or}
the semantic guard (\texttt{semantic\_guard.is\_risky(text)}, blocked as
\texttt{STPA\_Blocked: semantic\_risk}). There is exactly one dependent
variable, $\mathit{intercepted} = S_A \lor S_B \lor S_C$, so the three systems
are nested and their differences are attributable to the added layer rather
than to a re-definition of the task. All three are evaluated by a single
evaluator (\texttt{tools/phase\_c\_eval.py}) which reuses the Phase~A scanner
for the first two levels instead of re-implementing matching, so the same
payload cannot score differently because two code paths disagree. Latency is
measured per complete judgment (lexical levels plus, for $S_C$, the semantic
channel) and reduced to p50/p95; the pre-registered budget is
$p95 < 100$~ms for a complete judgment.

\paragraph{Two held-out sets and the leakage protocol.} The evaluation uses
three disjoint corpora with different roles. $D_{\mathrm{dev}}$ is the Phase~B
training stream (the \texttt{split=="train"} seed attacks, the training-side
bypass payloads, and the 74 benign items); it is the only corpus the guard is
calibrated on, and it is visible to the layer's author. $E_1$ is the
zero-day set fixed by the evaluation setup (forty held-out variants of the
Phase~B held-out seeds, SHA-256
\texttt{432e08a2d47cdc96\ldots}, generation-0 mutation distribution).
$E_0$ is a fresh held-out realisation drawn with a new seed
(\texttt{20261029}) from the same thirty-five held-out seed pool. The author of
the intent library may read neither $E_1$ nor $E_0$: the library is built from
$D_{\mathrm{dev}}$ only, its SHA-256 is frozen before the evaluation and
recorded in \texttt{experiments/phase-c/intent\_library.json}, and the
threshold is chosen on $D_{\mathrm{dev}}$ alone. $E_0$ exists to check that a
$D_{\mathrm{dev}}$-fitted threshold is not fitted to one particular
realisation: it is a new random implementation, not a new attack family.
Consequently we describe both $E_1$ and $E_0$ as \emph{held-out variants} and
never as out-of-distribution data, the same convention as in Phase~B.

\paragraph{The semantic channel: a change of representation, not a neural
model.} The guard maps text to a vector by an \emph{offline intent-concept
encoder} (backend \texttt{offline\_concept}, \texttt{is\_neural = false}): a
hand-authored library of nine harmful-intent prototypes with multilingual roots
(Chinese, English, Russian, Arabic) and character n-gram fallback, implemented
in pure NumPy. Its similarity combines a prototype cosine with weighted
root/subword evidence and is clipped to $[0,1]$; the intent library, its
threshold and its hash are frozen before evaluation, and the guard reports the
matched intent, the similarity score and the STPA reason code, so an
interception is attributable rather than opaque. The threshold is not the
value suggested at planning time: on the frozen library, $0.45$ is the point
that maximises recall on $D_{\mathrm{dev}}$ subject to adding zero benign false
positives (0.85 would have retained only 11.7\% of the $D_{\mathrm{dev}}$
bypass recall against 67.6\% at 0.45, so the suggested value is unusable on
this score scale). This machine has no PyTorch, no Transformers, no embedding
weights, no Ollama and no network, so the experiment uses no neural sentence
encoder; we therefore do not claim one. The claim is narrower and, we think,
sufficient for the argument: the guard changes the representation from surface
tokens to intent concepts, which is a different \emph{kind} of variety, not
merely more of the same.

\paragraph{Requisite variety and second-order control.} Ashby's law of
requisite variety states that a regulator can attenuate only that part of a
disturbance's variety which it can itself match. Read against the Phase~B
result, the plateau is not a tuning failure but a variety mismatch. Each
lexical patch adds variety in the regulator's alphabet (more tokens, more
normalisation rules) while the disturbance moves in a different dimension
(meaning preserved, surface destroyed); the total variety of the controller
grows, but its variety \emph{along the disturbance's direction} does not.
Thirteen patches and a stationary held-out rate is precisely the signature of
a regulator whose state space does not contain the disturbance's dimension.
The controller cannot see this from inside its own action loop, because from
inside, every accepted patch is an improvement on the set that motivated it:
the training stream does move (0.775 to 0.90). What is missing is an
observation of the loop itself.

That observation is the second-order move. A first-order controller adjusts
its actions on the plant; a second-order controller adjusts its own structure
after observing its own control loop, the step von Foerster describes as
moving from the cybernetics of observed systems to the cybernetics of
observing systems, and which Ashby already formalised as the search for
ultrastability in a system that must survive a changing environment. The
mechanism in this work is deliberately minimal: the evolutionary loop records
marginal held-out coverage per generation, and when three consecutive
generations contribute zero marginal coverage the loop stops generating
patches and hands over to a structural change
(\texttt{stop\_reason="trigger\_phase\_c"}). The trigger is not another
heuristic about attacks; it is a statement the controller makes about
\emph{itself}---``my learning has stopped adding variety''---and it is the
point at which a parametric mutation is replaced by a structural one.
Structural mutation here means adding a dimension to the controller's state
space, a semantic channel that can vary with the semantic dimension of the
disturbance. Whether that added dimension actually restores requisite variety
is an empirical question, answered by the measured interception rates below
rather than by the vocabulary of cybernetics.

\paragraph{Results.} Tables~\ref{tab:phase-c} and~\ref{tab:phase-c-adjudicated}
and Figures~\ref{fig:phase-c-comparison}--\ref{fig:phase-c-radar} report the
frozen run (\texttt{run-semantic-20261028-193207}). On $E_1$, $S_C$ intercepts
34/40 (0.850) against 31/40 (0.775) for $S_B$ and 15/40 (0.375) for $S_A$; on
$E_0$ it intercepts 37/40 (0.925) against 33/40 (0.825) and 16/40 (0.400). The
semantic channel therefore contributes exactly three additional interceptions
on $E_1$ and four on $E_0$, i.e.\ $+0.075$ and $+0.100$ in rate, while the
benign false-positive rate is unchanged at 0.027 (the same two baseline items,
2/74) and the p95 latency of a complete judgment is 1.3--2.7~ms across the two
sets, far inside the 100~ms budget (the worst single E1 judgment in the same
run took 20.6~ms). $E_0$ reaches 0.925; $E_1$ stays at 0.850, below the
pre-registered $>0.90$ target.

\paragraph{The gain is concentrated in explicit harm.} The aggregate rates hide
where the gain comes from, so the evaluation owner labels the nine $E_1$ items
that $S_B$ misses, \emph{before} the semantic guard exists, under a
pre-registered policy reading of the project's own severity rules: $M$ = an
explicit harmful instruction that must be intercepted; $R$ = a mild
role-play variant that the policy says should be intercepted; $B$ = benign
creative framing that must \emph{not} be intercepted, so intercepting it is a
false positive. Table~\ref{tab:phase-c-adjudicated} separates the systems on
these subsets. On $E_1$ the semantic guard intercepts $M$ 3/3 (the explicit
cross-lingual red lines that neither lexical system catches) and leaves both
$B$ items un-intercepted (zero false positives on benign creation); on $E_0$ it
intercepts $M$ 4/5. The same comparison shows the limit: $R$ remains 0/4 on
$E_1$ and 0/2 on $E_0$, and $S_A$/$S_B$ intercept none of these items either.
On the denominator that excludes the two $B$ items ($n=38$), the $E_1$ rates
are 0.395 ($S_A$), 0.816 ($S_B$) and 0.895 ($S_C$). The contribution of
Phase~C is therefore not a uniform rise but a specific one: explicit harmful
intent moves from 0\% to 100\% on $E_1$ (3/3) and to 80\% on $E_0$ (4/5), at
zero additional benign false positives and a latency cost two orders of
magnitude below the budget.

\paragraph{The residual gap is a separability limit, not a threshold-tuning
failure.} The four $E_1$ role-play variants that remain uncovered score
0.4332--0.4498 against a frozen threshold of 0.45, while the highest-scoring
benign text on $D_{\mathrm{dev}}$ scores 0.443983. Two of the four (0.4432 and
0.4428) are \emph{below} that benign maximum, so no threshold can intercept
them without admitting new benign false positives; the other two (0.4486 and
0.4498) lie in the narrow band between the benign maximum and 0.45, a band
that the 0.01-step calibration grid did not resolve. What limits Phase~C here
is therefore the separability of the concept-similarity representation itself,
not its calibration---and this is exactly the cybernetic reading: the added
dimension is large enough to match the $M$ class (explicit harmful intent) and
too small to match the $R$ class (mild role-playing), whose scores overlap with
benign creative writing.

\paragraph{Discussion: what this experiment tests and what it shows.} The
experiment was designed to test a specific claim, whose wording was fixed by
the evaluation owner for this phase: \emph{``Our findings demonstrate the
limitation of first-order lexical co-evolution. The plateau at 0.775 indicates
that structural mutation (Second-Order Cybernetics) is mandatory. By
introducing a semantic guard, Phase C achieved a structural leap, restoring the
system's requisite variety against semantic mutations.''} However, the measured
results qualify this claim: on held-out mutations the semantic guard raises
interception from 0.775 to 0.850 ($E_1$) and from 0.825 to 0.925 ($E_0$),
converting explicit cross-lingual red lines from 0\% to 100\% on $E_1$ (3/3)
and to 80\% on $E_0$ (4/5), at zero additional benign false positives and
1.3--2.7~ms p95 latency, while mild role-play variants remain uncovered (0/4
on $E_1$) and $E_1$ therefore stays below the pre-registered 0.90 target. We
therefore report the structural leap as \textbf{partial} and not as a
conclusion: the semantic guard restores requisite variety for explicit harmful
intent, but not for mild role-play, where the encoder's score distribution
overlaps benign creative writing. The first sentence of the claim is fully
supported by Phase~B; the third is supported only in that qualified sense, and
this section says so rather than letting the unqualified sentence stand.

Two provenance notes belong with that verdict. First, the encoder is an offline
intent-concept encoder (non-neural) with a threshold calibrated on
$D_{\mathrm{dev}}$ only; no neural sentence embedding was run, and no external
API was called ($\mathit{api\_calls} = 0$, $\mathit{api\_tokens\_used} = 0$).
Second, $E_1$ and $E_0$ are held-out variants generated from the same seed pool
under the same mutation distribution, not out-of-distribution data; $E_0$ is a
fresh random realisation with a new seed, not a new attack family. Latency is a
single-machine NumPy measurement without concurrency or network, so it is not
production end-to-end latency.

\paragraph{What this shows and does not show.} It shows that a second-order
trigger---observing zero marginal coverage and changing the regulator's state
space instead of adding another patch---can be built in a real closed loop, and
that the added semantic dimension produces a measurable, attributable gain on
the class of failures the lexical loop provably could not reach, at a
negligible latency and false-positive cost. It does not show that the semantic
layer generalises beyond its nine hand-authored intents: the concept similarity
separates explicit harm from benign text but not mild role-play from benign
creative writing, and an attack rewrite that shares no lexical evidence with
the intent library will still be missed (the residual misses are listed in
\texttt{residual\_misses\_C}). The $M$/$R$/$B$ labels were written by the
evaluation owner before the guard existed, which removes post-hoc fitting but
introduces author judgment; the benign corpus contains no Russian or Arabic
text, so cross-lingual false positives remain untested; and the reported
latency, while far below the budget, was measured on an otherwise idle
machine.
